## 1. Scope statement test

JIS Aims & Scope (official page, Wayback 2024-06-08) covers managing, sustaining, enhancing and transforming civil infrastructure systems, explicitly including **energy production and distribution**, management problems "fraught with **uncertain information**", and solutions requiring "sound engineering judgment, **economic flexibility**…". Papers must have "a well-defined engineering component". The manuscript's object (renewal-time re-layout of an energy-distribution network under engineering feasibility gates, evaluated economically, with incomplete records) is inside this statement. The scope test is necessary but not sufficient, so publication precedents were checked.

## 3. Answer: does JIS demonstrably publish research of this methodological and substantive type?

**Yes, with qualification.**

- *Substantive type*: JIS publishes renewal/rehabilitation decision research (Hastak and Gokhale 2000; Khan et al. 2015; Dao et al. 2019), integrated municipal and distribution-network asset management (Abu-Samra et al. 2020; Kerwin and Adey 2021), and power-distribution infrastructure planning (Salman and Li 2017; Bynum et al. 2021). Recent work co-authored by the Editor in Chief addresses decision support under data scarcity (Dehghani et al. 2025).
- *Methodological type*: JIS publishes flexibility valuation for utility networks (Basupi and Kapelan 2015; Hadjidemetriou et al. 2022), real-options valuation for underground systems (Zahed et al. 2020), synthetic reconstruction of hidden utility networks from road graphs (Fletcher et al. 2026), and engineering-constrained network optimisation (Bynum et al. 2021).
- *No JIS paper found* evaluates renewal-time topology re-layout of low-voltage distribution networks with an incumbent-admissible economic estimand. This is the gap the manuscript can claim; it is narrow.

**Fit decision: GO (credible fit).** `SCOPE NO-GO` is not triggered.

## 4. Novelty constraints imposed by the JIS literature

1. Valuing design flexibility is established (Basupi and Kapelan 2015). The manuscript must not claim to introduce flexibility valuation; it introduces a renewal-window, incumbent-admissible, feasibility-gated **screening estimand** and reports its empirical distribution.
2. Road-graph reconstruction of hidden networks is established (Fletcher et al. 2026; Abdel-Mottaleb and Zhang 2020). Reconstruction is presented as an enabling, conditional input; the lack of utility ground truth is a primary limitation.
3. Engineering-constrained power-network optimisation already appears in JIS (Bynum et al. 2021); the Steiner-tree candidate generator is not claimed as a methodological advance.
4. The JIS precedents Basupi and Kapelan 2015, Fletcher et al. 2026, Dehghani et al. 2025, Kerwin and Adey 2021, Dao et al. 2019, Hastak and Gokhale 2000, Salman and Li 2017 and Zahed et al. 2020 are cited in the revised introduction/discussion where they support a specific claim (not as padding).
